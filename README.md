# Aspen

**NOTE:** Aspen is very much a concept at this point. Much of what's written is aspirational.

Aspen helps you keep the things you already use—notes, documents, links, files, projects—**connected instead of scattered across tools**.

You don’t have to replace your existing tools, you keep using them and Aspen brings everything together so that context, relationships, and meaning don’t get lost over time.

> _Aspen trees look like many separate trees but are actually connected underground by a shared root system. Aspen works the same way: your tools stay separate, but the roots—the connections between your work—are shared._

---

## Why Aspen exists

Most tools aren't designed to work together. Over time, this creates familiar problems:

- You remember _something_ exists, but not where it is.
- Notes, documents, conversations, and links are disorganized and scattered everywhere.
- The same idea ends up copied into multiple tools.
- Switching tools means losing the thread of your thinking.

Aspen exists to solve **context loss**. It gives you a place to link, explore, and revisit the relationships between the things you already use—without forcing you to move everything into a new system.

**You don’t migrate to Aspen. You add it.**

Aspen is not a new “home” for all your work.

- Keep using Notion, Obsidian, your editor, Google Docs, files on disk, bookmarks—whatever already works for you.
- Share whatever you're working on with Aspen to link everything together.
- Use incredibly powerful search features to find anything you share.

You might use Aspen to:

- link a design decision to the code, documents, and discussions behind it
- connect meeting notes to the files and links that were referenced
- revisit old work and understand _why_ it mattered, not just _what_ it was

Aspen gives your existing workflow memory.

---

## Collaboration, without giving up control

Aspen supports sharing and collaboration, but with a different default:

- Your data lives on your devices.
- Sharing is explicit and predictable—you always know who you’re sharing with.
- There’s no central service that owns or analyzes your content.

You can collaborate directly with other people while keeping clear boundaries between personal, shared, and private work.

---

## Design principles (high level)

Aspen is built around a few non-negotiable principles:

- **Local-first**: it works offline; sync is additive
- **Privacy by default**: all data is encrypted end-to-end
- **No required backend**: servers are optional, never authoritative
- **Simple sharing**: permissions are understandable and enforceable
- **Composable**: multiple apps can build on the same data
